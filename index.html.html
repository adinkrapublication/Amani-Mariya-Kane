About the Book

A landmark work of documented testimony

For twenty years, one woman navigated the Swiss justice system. A Black woman. An economist with a doctorate. A former international banking professional. A property owner on the Gold Coast of Zurich — one of the most expensive addresses in Europe.

Over 200 complaints filed. Two illegal evictions. Eight tonnes of personal belongings destroyed. Legal costs exceeding CHF 400,000. And a property sold at auction for a fraction of its value, through an enforcement office that falsified documents.

The Swiss Illusion is the documented account of what happened. But it is more than a personal testimony. It is a rigorous analytical work drawing on reports from GRECO, the United Nations, the European Court of Human Rights, and twenty years of academic research from Swiss universities.

Switzerland ranks consistently among the top countries in global rule-of-law indices. The Swiss Illusion examines that ranking against the evidence — and finds it does not hold.

Key Findings

What experts, institutions &
international bodies found

01
Judicial Politicisation
83% of Swiss judges say the mandat tax undermines their credibility. Judges pay up to CHF 22,000 annually to their political party to keep their seat. GRECO has condemned this system repeatedly since 2020.
02
No Independent Oversight
Switzerland reached 2025 without any disciplinary mechanism for federal judges. The parliament that elects judges also oversees them — a structural conflict of interest incompatible with European standards.
03
Institutional Racism
The UN (2022) documented systematic racial profiling. The ECHR unanimously condemned Switzerland in Wa Baile v. Switzerland (2024) for racial discrimination and the absence of any effective domestic remedy.
04
The Invisible Bench
In 20 years of proceedings, not one non-white European judge encountered. Switzerland publishes ethnic crime statistics annually — but has never studied the ethnic composition of its judiciary. This is not oversight. It is choice.
05
Justice as a Weapon
A complete civil procedure costs CHF 5,000–50,000. The Federal Tribunal declared a case inadmissible for failure to pay CHF 800. Legal aid is denied in over 70% of applications. The system protects those who can pay — not those who are right.
06
The Invisible Hand
Everyone knows. The judges know. The parliamentarians know. GRECO returns year after year. Nothing changes — because those with the power to change the system have no incentive to do so. This is not conspiracy. It is institutional capture.

About the Author

Amani Mariya Kane

Amani Mariya Kane is an economist with a doctorate and a former international banking professional. She lived and worked in Switzerland for over twenty years.

The Swiss Illusion is her first book, published under a pen name — an irony she accepts with clarity: in a country where justice is denied through opacity, one must sometimes speak truth through a veil.

The silence ends here.
